## 1. The most significant improvements

### 1.1 There are two free official tests, not three. Mock 2 was going to be a repeat

**Checked.** I downloaded the digital sample's answer keys, both paper sample zips and the handbook, and compared them.

| File | Reading and Use of English key | Listening key |
|---|---|---|
| Digital sample | B C C A B B C D … | A B C A C A, report, fire … |
| Paper Sample 1 | identical, all 56 answers | identical, all 30 answers |
| Paper Sample 2 | different | different |
| Handbook | contains the answers of **both** Sample 1 and Sample 2; the zips' answer-key PDFs are handbook pages | same |

So the digital sample, paper Sample 1 and the handbook's first paper are one test (**Test A**), and paper Sample 2 and the handbook's second paper are the other (**Test B**).

The old plan ran the diagnostic on the digital sample, Mock 1 on Sample 2 and Mock 2 on "the handbook sample". Mock 2 would have repeated one of the first two, and its score would have looked like progress.

**New mock strategy:** Test A is the diagnostic (Days 2–4). Mock 1 (Day 13) is unofficial, on engexam. **Test B is sealed until Day 19 and used once, as Mock 2 on Day 20.** That puts the two official readings 17 days apart, which is the most the material allows.

### 1.2 Writing now has an official reference point

**Checked.** Each sample's answer-key PDF includes six real candidate scripts with examiner marks and comments on all four criteria (totals from 9/20 to 17/20). The old plan calibrated ChatGPT against "a marked sample in the writing guide" without checking what the guide holds.

- Day 5: ChatGPT marks two Sample 1 scripts blind, and you record how far it is from the examiner (prompt GPT-1b).
- Day 5: you rank your own diagnostic essay against the three marked essays on the same task.
- Day 21: the same ranking against the Test B scripts, after Mock 2.

A rank against examiner-marked scripts is still not a mark, but it is the only Writing evidence in the plan that does not depend on ChatGPT.

### 1.3 Part 2 preparation is narrowed to two genres plus one backup

The Writing paper sets three Part 2 questions from four task types (**Checked** in both samples; not a published guarantee). Two prepared genres should therefore always leave you one. The plan now trains **proposal and report**, which share one template, and one backup (review or letter). The old plan spread single attempts across proposal, review, report and letter.

### 1.4 A redundant full Writing paper is gone

Day 18 was a 90-minute Writing paper two days before Mock 2, which contains a 90-minute Writing paper. It is now one timed backup-genre text (45 min) plus a flex block. Writing practice falls from twelve timed texts to eleven and stays the largest share of the plan.

### 1.5 Flexible time is larger and is allocated by a rule

The old plan had 50 minutes of flex in 26 days and chose the skill through a ChatGPT "weekly review". It now has 75 minutes in three blocks plus the week-4 fix list, and the choice takes five minutes with no AI (STUDY_PLAN §4):

1. Gap per skill = Secure line minus latest result, as a percentage of the maximum.
2. Weakest skill gets the first flex block, second weakest the next.
3. Ties go to Use of English, then Listening, Reading, Writing.
4. Inside the skill, practise the part where you lost the most **marks**, not the lowest percentage.

Guards cover Speaking (flex only if a band is weak), Writing as flex skill (a plan and one paragraph, not another full text), and the case where the diagnostic shows Writing is not the problem (two writing sessions shrink to plans and 80 minutes move to the weak objective skills).

### 1.6 Score tracking separates three classes of result

| Class | Source | Recorded as |
|---|---|---|
| Official anchor | Reading, Use of English, Listening on Test A and Test B only | Raw score against the 160/180/200 anchors |
| Unofficial practice | All Flo-Joe and engexam work, including Mock 1 | Raw and percentage, trend only |
| Tutor assessment | All Writing and Speaking marks | Writing: four 0–5 bands per task. Speaking: five 0–5 bands |

Speaking is no longer recorded "out of 75". **Checked** in the conversion guide: Pronunciation carries 10 of the 75 marks and ChatGPT cannot judge it, so a total would be invented. The Secure / Borderline / Priority labels are now fixed raw-score lines instead of "about ±10%".

### 1.7 Tutor workflows

- **Writing (GPT-1, 1b, 2, 3):** blind calibration; the lower band when in doubt; every band backed by a quotation; a content checklist before any band; a fixed list of thirteen error types so notebook entries can be counted; a new chat per text so earlier marks do not anchor later ones; the handbook is kept out of the ChatGPT Project so Test B cannot leak back as a "fresh" prompt.
- **Mistake review (GPT-8, GPT-5):** GPT-8 is now a retest on new sentences, which checks that a rule transfers and not only that a card is remembered. Misses go back to a one-day review. The three most frequent error types become a proofreading card for the last eight minutes of each Writing task.
- **Speaking (GPT-6, GPT-7):** simulations on Days 5 and 20 use the official Speaking booklets, with real pictures and the real interlocutor script. The old prompt had ChatGPT describe pictures in words. New "shadow tests" use the three official videos: pause after each task, answer aloud, then watch the candidates and read their examiner marks. A self-check list is applied to each recording.

### 1.8 Smaller corrections

- Reading Part 6 (8 marks) was practised once outside the mocks. Day 11 now has a second Part 6 in place of a Part 8.
- Day 6 trimmed from 120 to 100 minutes.
- Missed-day rules gained a priority order for what to keep, a 45-minute daily cap on catch-up, and a rule for losing three or more days in a row.
- All 62 distinct links in both documents return HTTP 200 (checked by script; this proves the pages exist, not that each Flo-Joe page is the exact part named).

| Speaking | about 130 | 7% | 20% |
| Flex (weakest skills) | 75 | 4% | — |
| Notebook review, error analysis, admin | about 330 | outside the split | — |

Total is roughly 37 hours over 26 days. Ordinary days run 80–90 minutes. Five days exceed your stated 75–90: Days 2 and 4 (one 90-minute paper each), Day 6 (100) and the two mock Saturdays (about 4 h 15 and 4 h 30).

**Judgement on the Writing share.** 42% of time for 20% of the score is deliberate but only right if Writing really is the weak skill. Each text costs 45 minutes to write and 25 to close, and it is your reported weakness. The diagnostic decides: if Writing comes out Borderline or better while two objective papers are Priority, the guard in STUDY_PLAN §4 moves 80 minutes out of Writing. Speaking is kept at 7% because it is your strong skill and its practice is cheap.

## 4. Corrections for the implementation session

UX_SPEC.md and CLAUDE_CODE_HANDOFF.md predate this audit. Where they disagree with STUDY_PLAN.md, the plan wins. The points below are the known conflicts.

1. **Official sources.** The handoff says to flag "the digital sample, paper Sample 2, the handbook sample" as official. The handbook is not a test source. Official results come from exactly four tasks: `d02-a`, `d03-a`, `d20-a`, `d20-c`. Mock 1 (`d13-*`) is unofficial.
2. **Result class needs three values, not a boolean.** Replace `official: boolean` with something like `basis: 'official' | 'unofficial' | 'tutor'`. A Writing mark on an official task is still `tutor`.
3. **Speaking has no /75 field.** Record five bands (Grammatical Resource, Lexical Resource, Discourse Management, Interactive Communication, Global Achievement), 0–5 in steps of 0.5. No total, no bar against the anchors. Remove "Speaking 75" from the score maxima.
4. **Writing is recorded per criterion.** Four whole bands 0–5 per task (Content, Communicative Achievement, Organisation, Language); /20 and /40 are derived. Shown with the "Tutor estimate. Not examiner-marked." caption and never drawn as a position on the official anchor scale.
5. **Labels use fixed lines.** Secure / Borderline / Priority come from the table in STUDY_PLAN §2, replacing the "within 10% of the anchor" rule in UX_SPEC §8.
6. **Task ids changed.** New: `d07-d`, `d18-c`. Changed meaning: `d05-a`, `d05-c`, `d06-c`, `d09-d`, `d11-b`, `d11-d`, `d12-d`, all of Day 13, `d14-b`, `d14-c`, `d15-c`, `d16-c`, `d16-d`, all of Days 18–21. Re-transcribe every day from the plan; do not patch an earlier seed. The data test "exactly 26 days, unique ids" still holds.
7. **Prompts changed.** GPT-1b is new. GPT-6 has two prompts (mode A, mode B). GPT-7 is now mostly a method with one short prompt. GPT-8 is a mistake retest, not a weekly review. GPT-9 has two prompts (single task, full paper). Prompt ids need to allow for this, for example `gpt-1b`, `gpt-6a`, `gpt-6b`, `gpt-9a`, `gpt-9b`.
8. **Mistake entries carry an error type** from the fixed list in GPT-1 (thirteen values). Use it as the suggestion list for the existing subcategory field. A GPT-8 miss is the existing "Missed" action.
9. **Flex tasks** (`d10-d`, `d15-b`, `d18-c`) need somewhere to hold the week's chosen skill and part. A one-line note is enough.
10. **Test B must not be exposed early.** The Library can list it, labelled "sealed until 4 Dec". No Test B task wording, topic or answer may appear in the app's text. The same goes for all exam content, as already specified.
11. **Catch-up overflow hint** should quote the plan's new keep-order (Mock 2 first, Speaking practice last), not "drop the oldest".
12. **The plan is already running.** Day 1 was 16 November; the diagnostic starts on the 17th whether or not the app exists.
