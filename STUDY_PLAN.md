## 1. How the plan works

**An ordinary day is 75–90 minutes in four blocks:**

| Block | Minutes | What |
|---|---|---|
| Review | 10 | Mistake-notebook items due today. Cover the correction, produce it, check |
| Timed block A | 20–30 | A timed exam part, answers checked immediately |
| Block B | 30–45 | Writing cycle step, or a second timed part |
| Error log | 5–10 | Add today's errors to the notebook, record scores |

**Rules that apply every day**

1. Timed, typed, on screen, **spell-check and autocorrect off**.
2. Never leave a wrong answer unexplained. For each: why the right answer is right, why yours was wrong.
3. Only errors you could repeat go into the notebook (not careless slips). Three to eight a day is normal.
4. Writing always runs in a two-step cycle: **write timed**, then **diagnose → self-correct → rewrite**.
5. Ask ChatGPT for hints before answers (§6).

**Reference timings** (exam pace to train toward)

| Paper | Budget |
|---|---|
| Use of English Parts 1–4 | 30–35 min total: about 7 / 8 / 8 / 10 |
| Reading Parts 5–8 | 55 min total: about 15 / 12 / 15 / 13 |
| Writing | 45 min per task: 7 plan, 30 write, 8 check |
| Listening | Fixed by the audio; use the pre-reading time to underline key words and predict |

**Score interpretation for practice** (see RESEARCH §3). Every number you record belongs to one of three classes, and they are never mixed:

| Class | Comes from | Record as |
|---|---|---|
| **Official anchor** | Reading, Use of English, Listening on TEST A (Days 2–3) and TEST B (Day 20) | Raw /50, /28, /30, compared with the 160/180/200 anchors |
| **Unofficial practice** | Every Flo-Joe and engexam score, including Mock 1 | Raw and percentage. Trend only, no anchors |
| **Tutor assessment** | Every Writing and Speaking mark from ChatGPT | Writing: four 0–5 bands per task. Speaking: five 0–5 bands, never a mark out of 75 |

On official material the C1 anchor is roughly 57–64%. Working targets: **anchor + 10 percentage points to feel safe for a pass, around 80% for Grade B territory** (an estimate, not a Cambridge figure).

Resource keys used below:
**DIG / TEST A** = official digital sample. It is the same test as paper Sample 1 and the first sample paper in the handbook · **S1** = [paper Sample 1 zip](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/Images/174025-advanced-2015-sample-papers-1.zip), used only for its Speaking booklet and its examiner-marked Writing scripts, and only after Day 4 · **TEST B** = [official paper Sample 2](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/Images/178484-advanced-2015-sample-papers-2.zip), the only other official test. **Sealed until Day 19**: do not unzip it, and do not browse the sample-paper pages of the handbook, which reprint it · **VID 1, 2, 3** = the three official Speaking test videos on the [preparation page](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/exams-and-tests/qualifications/advanced/preparation/), in this order: Pedro and Isabela, Teresa and Antonio, then the group of three (Marta, Giovanna and Anna); each has a PDF of examiner comments linked in RESEARCH §9 · **FJ** = Flo-Joe · **ENG** = engexam.info · **GPT-n** = prompt n in §6.

## 2. Diagnostic process (Days 2–5)

All on the official digital sample, so the anchors in RESEARCH §3 apply and the interface is learned at the same time.

| Day | Paper | Record |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | Reading and Use of English, 90 min | Raw score per part; Reading /50; Use of English /28 |
| 3 | Listening, ~40 min | Raw per part; total /30 |
| 4 | Writing, 90 min, both tasks | The two texts; word counts; whether you finished |
| 5 | ChatGPT calibrated on examiner-marked scripts; Writing marked with GPT-2; Speaking simulation with GPT-6 | Writing bands per task, Speaking bands, calibration offset, top three issues for each |

The digital sample does not mark answers and hides them once you finish. **Write your answers on paper as you go**, or check them against the key before pressing finish. Copy your essays into a file before finishing.

**Reading the baseline.** Label each skill from this table. Reading, Use of English and Listening are official anchors here. Writing and Speaking are tutor assessments, so their thresholds sit about 10% higher to allow for a generous marker; move them by your Day 5 calibration offset if it is larger.

| Skill | Priority | Borderline | Secure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reading /50 | 31 or less | 32–36 | 37+ |
| Use of English /28 | 15 or less | 16–18 | 19+ |
| Listening /30 | 17 or less | 18–20 | 21+ |
| Writing /40 (tutor) | 27 or less | 28–31 | 32+ |
| Speaking, mean of five bands (tutor) | below 3.5 | 3.5–3.9 | 4.0+ |

Priority means below the 180 anchor. Secure means at least 10% of the maximum above it. For an unofficial whole-section result (Mock 1) use the same lines as percentages (Reading 64 / 74, Use of English 57 / 68, Listening 60 / 70) and treat the label as provisional.

Overall is an average, so one Priority skill does not mean failure if the others are Secure. It does mean that skill is where points are cheapest.

## 4. Adaptive rules

### Weekly allocation (five minutes, Days 7, 14 and 21)

1. **Latest result per skill.** Use the most recent whole-section result: Days 2–5 on Day 7, Mock 1 plus that week's tutor marks on Day 14, Mock 2 on Day 21.
2. **Gap.** For each skill, gap = Secure line (§2) minus your result, as a percentage of the maximum. Example: Use of English 14/28 against a Secure line of 19 is a gap of 18 points. A skill at or above its Secure line has no gap.
3. **Rank** the skills by gap. Gaps within 5 points of each other count as a tie; break ties in this order: Use of English, Listening, Reading, Writing. My judgement: Use of English responds fastest to drilling, and Writing already has the largest fixed share of the plan.
4. **Allocate the flex blocks.**

   | Week | Flex blocks | Goes to |
   |---|---|---|
   | 2 | `d10-d` (20 min) | Weakest skill |
   | 3 | `d15-b` (30 min), `d18-c` (25 min) | Weakest skill, then second weakest. If only one skill has a gap, it gets both |
   | 4 | `d22-c`, `d23-c` | The five-item fix list from Day 21 |

5. **Choose the part by marks lost, not by percentage.** Inside the flex skill, practise the part where your latest result dropped the most marks (maximum minus score). This weights Part 4 and Reading Parts 5–7 correctly, because their questions are worth two marks.
6. **Write it down** as one line: "Week 3 flex: Use of English Part 4, then Listening Part 2."

**Flex pool** (material no fixed day uses): ENG Reading and Use of English Tests 4 onward, one part at a time · ENG Listening Tests 5 onward · FJ Reading Test 1 Part 8 · GPT-4 for extra key word transformations.

**Guards**

| Situation | Rule |
|---|---|
| Speaking | Gets a flex block only if its mean band is below 3.5 or one band is 2.5 or lower. Then the block is a shadow test on the weak part |
| Writing is the flex skill | The block is one plan plus introduction plus one body paragraph (20–30 min) on a GPT-9 prompt, marked with GPT-2. Not another full text |
| Writing is Secure or Borderline on Day 5 **and** two of Reading, Use of English, Listening are Priority | Writing is over-served. `d15-c` becomes a ten-minute report plan, and `d18-b` becomes a ten-minute plan; the freed 80 minutes go to the two Priority skills |
| A skill is Secure twice in a row | Its next timed block outside a mock goes to the weakest skill instead |
| Listening is Priority | Add ten minutes of dictation from an ENG tapescript on the light days (12, 19) |
| No skill has a gap | Flex goes to the skill furthest below about 80% |

### Missed days

- The calendar never shifts. The exam date is fixed.
- A missed **[core]** task moves to a catch-up list. Do catch-up items before non-core tasks on the next ordinary day, or on Sunday. At most 45 minutes of catch-up in one day.
- A missed non-core task is dropped. No guilt, no backlog.
- More than three core tasks waiting: keep them in this order and drop from the bottom.
  1. Mock 2 on TEST B
  2. Closing an open writing cycle (GPT-2, self-correct, rewrite)
  3. Notebook review
  4. Mock 1 (may shrink to Reading and Use of English plus Listening, 2 h 15 min)
  5. Timed Use of English sets
  6. Flex blocks
  7. Speaking practice
- **Three or more days lost in a row:** do not try to catch up. Resume at today's date, carry over only items 1 and 2 from the list above, and re-run the weekly allocation.
- A missed mock moves to the next day. If neither weekend day works, split it across two weekday evenings. Never skip Mock 2.
- On a 30-minute day, do the listed Emergency version.

## 5. Mock strategy and reading the results

Only **two** independent official tests are free (RESEARCH §10, U3). The plan spends one at each end, so the two official readings are as far apart as possible.

| Event | When | Source | Result class |
|---|---|---|---|
| Diagnostic | Days 2–5, split | TEST A (official digital sample) | Official anchor for Reading, Use of English, Listening |
| Mock 1 | Sat 28 Nov, one sitting | engexam Test 1 + Listening Test 4, GPT-9 writing paper | Unofficial. Stamina, pacing, task choice |
| Mock 2 | Sat 5 Dec, one sitting plus Speaking | TEST B (official paper Sample 2), unseen | Official anchor. The readiness reading |
| Full Listening | Tue 8 Dec | engexam Listening Test 3 | Unofficial |

Rules that protect the mocks:

1. TEST A is never repeated as a test. Its paper form (Sample 1) and the handbook's first sample paper are the same questions.
2. TEST B is not opened before Day 19 and not read before Day 20. The handbook's second sample paper is the same test, so stay out of the handbook's sample-paper pages.
3. Day 25 reopens the digital sample for ten minutes of interface rehearsal only. Nothing is scored.

Two full mocks is the right number. A third would cost a Saturday better spent fixing what the second exposed, and there is no third official paper to make it meaningful.

**Honest reading of Mock 2** (official anchors for the three objective papers; tutor bands for the other two)

| Pattern | Reading |
|---|---|
| Reading, Use of English and Listening all at their Secure line or above; Writing and Speaking not Priority | Good position for a pass. Not a guarantee |
| Objective papers around the 180 anchors, one or two below | Uncertain. Final week goes entirely to the fix list |
| Two or more objective papers clearly below the anchor and Writing Priority | A pass is at risk. Decide about a backup test (RESEARCH §5) |
| Objective papers around 80%+ | Grade B is plausible; Writing and Speaking will decide it |

Never convert a mock into a single predicted scale score. Record raw scores against the anchors, and keep tutor bands apart from them.

## 6. ChatGPT tutor workflows

**Setup (Day 5).** Create one ChatGPT **Project** named "C1 Advanced". Paste GPT-1 as its instructions. Add two files to the Project if your plan allows uploads: the [writing guide](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/Images/600976-teacher-guide-for-writing-c1-advanced.pdf) and the [speaking guide](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/Images/735851-teacher-guide-for-speaking-c1-advanced.pdf). Do **not** upload the handbook or the Sample 2 zip: they contain TEST B, and ChatGPT may hand its tasks back to you as "fresh" prompts. Start a **new chat inside the Project for each text you have marked**, so an earlier mark does not anchor the next one; keep the rewrite check in the same chat as its first marking.

Three rules: you attempt first; hints before answers; every session ends with errors copied into the mistake notebook.
ChatGPT's marks are tutor assessments. It tends to be generous and can be wrong about what Cambridge accepts in Use of English. When it contradicts an official answer key or an examiner-marked script, the official source wins.

### GPT-1 · Tutor setup (once, as Project instructions)
```
You are my Cambridge C1 Advanced (CAE) tutor. My exam is the digital version on 12 December 2026. I am a Slovenian speaker at roughly B2+ aiming for an overall 180 at minimum and 193 if possible.

Rules for every reply:
1. Never give me an answer before I have attempted it. If I ask for the answer directly, give one hint first and ask me to try again. Give the answer only after two failed attempts or if I write "reveal".
2. When you correct me, label the error with exactly one of these types so I can log it: article, tense/aspect, preposition, collocation, word choice, word form, word order, agreement, punctuation, spelling, register, linking, task/content.
3. Mark as a Cambridge examiner would, not as an encouraging teacher. Whenever you hesitate between two bands, give the lower one. If something is only B2 level, say so. Do not praise.
4. Use the official Cambridge criteria. Writing: Content, Communicative Achievement, Organisation, Language, each a whole band 0-5. Speaking: Grammatical Resource, Lexical Resource, Discourse Management, Interactive Communication, Global Achievement, each 0-5. You cannot hear me well enough to mark Pronunciation, so never give it a band and never give a Speaking total.
5. Every band must be justified by quoting my own words. Never give me a Cambridge English Scale score, a grade, or a prediction of my exam result.
6. Keep replies short. No general study advice unless I ask.
7. Do not rewrite my text unless I explicitly ask for a model.
Confirm you understand in one sentence.
```

```

GENRE: [essay / proposal / report / review / letter or email]
```

## 7. Skill methods in brief

| Skill | Method |
|---|---|
| Use of English 1 | Read the whole text first. Decide by collocation and the word after the gap. Log every missed collocation as a full phrase |
| Use of English 2 | Identify the word class needed before thinking of a word. Most answers are grammar words |
| Use of English 3 | Decide part of speech, then check for negative and plural. Reread the sentence with your word in it. Check spelling twice |
| Use of English 4 | Underline what is missing from the second sentence. Key word unchanged. Count the words. A half-right answer still earns one mark |
| Reading 5 | Read the question stem, find the place, decide the answer in your own words, then look at the options |
| Reading 6 | Make a small grid on scrap paper: writers A–D against the topics, with + / − for their opinion |
| Reading 7 | Read the base text first. Mark pronouns, linkers and time references at the edges of every gap and every paragraph option. One option is extra |
| Reading 8 | Read the questions first and highlight key words; scan the texts for paraphrases, not for identical words |
| Listening | Read ahead in every pause. First hearing: answer. Second: confirm and fill gaps. Part 2: write exactly what you hear, check spelling and singular/plural |
| Writing | Plan seven minutes. One idea per paragraph. Decide the register from the reader named in the task. Proofread for your personal top three errors |
| Writing Part 2 choice | Three questions are set from four task types, so two prepared types should always leave you one. **Main pair: proposal and report** (one shared template). **Backup: one of review or letter.** Do not spread practice across all four |
| Speaking | Part 2: compare, speculate ("it might be that…"), answer both questions. Part 3: invite, respond, build; do not rush to the decision |
| Mistake notebook | One line per error: wrong → correct → rule → error type. Daily: cover the correction and produce it (1, 3, 7 days). Weekly when time allows: GPT-8 retest on new sentences. Day 21: GPT-5 for patterns. Your three most frequent error types become your proofreading card |
