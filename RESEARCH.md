## 2. Exam structure

Sources: [Cambridge C1 Advanced preparation page][prep] (digital sample test instructions) **[Confirmed]** for paper lengths, question counts and mark weights; part-level detail **[Established]** and arithmetically consistent with the [score conversion guide][conv].

### Reading and Use of English — 90 minutes, 8 parts, 56 questions

| Part | Task | Qs | Marks each | Counts toward |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Multiple-choice cloze | 8 | 1 | Reading |
| 2 | Open cloze | 8 | 1 | Use of English |
| 3 | Word formation | 8 | 1 | Use of English |
| 4 | Key word transformations (3–6 words) | 6 | up to 2 | Use of English |
| 5 | Long text, multiple choice | 6 | 2 | Reading |
| 6 | Cross-text multiple matching | 4 | 2 | Reading |
| 7 | Gapped text | 6 | 2 | Reading |
| 8 | Multiple matching | 10 | 1 | Reading |

Reading = Parts 1, 5, 6, 7, 8 = **50 marks**. Use of English = Parts 2, 3, 4 = **28 marks**. **[Confirmed]** in the conversion guide.
Consequence: Parts 5–7 are 32 of the 50 Reading marks from only 16 questions. One wrong answer there costs 2 marks.

### Writing — 90 minutes, 2 tasks, equal marks

- Part 1: compulsory essay, 220–260 words, discussing two of three given points.
- Part 2: choose one of three: letter/email, proposal, report, review. 220–260 words.
- **Consequence [Estimate]:** three questions are set from four task types, so being well prepared for any **two** types should leave at least one of them on the paper. Proposal and report share almost all conventions (title, headings, neutral-formal register, recommendations), so they are the cheapest pair. Both official samples follow this three-from-four pattern; it is not a published guarantee, so keep one backup type (review or letter).
- Each task marked 0–5 on four subscales: **Content, Communicative Achievement, Organisation, Language** → 20 per task, **40 total**.

### Listening — about 40 minutes, 4 parts, 30 questions, 1 mark each

Part 1: three short extracts, 6 multiple-choice. Part 2: sentence completion, 8. Part 3: long interview, 6 multiple-choice. Part 4: two parallel matching tasks, 10. Every recording is played twice. **[Confirmed]**

### Speaking — about 15 minutes, in pairs (sometimes a group of three), two examiners

Part 1 interview (~2 min) · Part 2 individual long turn comparing two of three pictures, 1 minute each plus a 30-second response to the partner (~4 min) · Part 3 collaborative task, 2 minutes discussion + 1 minute decision (~4 min) · Part 4 discussion (~5 min).
The assessor marks **Grammatical Resource, Lexical Resource, Discourse Management, Pronunciation, Interactive Communication** (0–5 each, doubled); the interlocutor gives a **Global Achievement** mark (0–5, multiplied by five). Total **75**; half marks are possible. **[Confirmed]** in the [conversion guide][conv]. The 180 anchor of 45/75 therefore equals an average of band 3 on every criterion.
Face to face, identical to the paper-based exam. **[Confirmed]** ([Digital FAQ][faq])

## 3. Scoring

Source: [Converting practice test scores to Cambridge English Scale scores][conv] **[Confirmed]**.

- Five scores are reported: Reading, Use of English, Writing, Listening, Speaking.
- **Overall = (sum of the five) ÷ 5, rounded.** Each is worth 20%.
- **No minimum per paper.** The grade comes from the overall score, so a strong skill compensates for a weak one.
- Grades **[Established]**: 200–210 Grade A (certificate states C2) · 193–199 Grade B · 180–192 Grade C · 160–179 "Level B2" certificate · 142–159 reported, no certificate.

### Official practice-score anchors (official Cambridge practice tests only)

| Skill | Max | Scale 160 (B2) | Scale 180 (C1) | Scale 200 (C2) | % needed for 180 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Reading | 50 | 23 | **32** | 43 | 64% |
| Use of English | 28 | 11 | **16** | 23 | 57% |
| Writing | 40 | 16 | **24** | 34 | 60% |
| Listening | 30 | 13 | **18** | 26 | 60% |
| Speaking | 75 | 30 | **45** | 66 | 60% |

Cambridge's own caveats, which the app must respect:
1. The tables apply **only to official Cambridge practice tests**.
2. They indicate readiness and relative strengths; they "should not be used to try to predict precise scores in the live exam".
3. Thresholds vary between test versions. Treat anything within **±3 scale points** of a boundary as uncertain. A practice score barely above 180 does not mean a pass.

**[Estimate]** There is no official raw-score anchor for Grade B (193). Linear interpolation between the 180 and 200 anchors gives roughly Reading 39/50, Use of English 20–21/28, Writing 30–31/40, Listening 23/30, Speaking 59/75. This is arithmetic, not a Cambridge figure, and must be labelled as such wherever it appears.

### Three classes of result — never mix them

| Class | What it is | How to record | May be compared with the anchors? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Official anchor** | Reading, Use of English or Listening from an official Cambridge test, taken timed and marked with the official key | Raw score out of 50 / 28 / 30 | Yes, with the ±3 caveat |
| **Unofficial practice** | Any Flo-Joe or engexam score, part or whole | Raw score and percentage | No. Trend only |
| **Tutor assessment** | Any Writing or Speaking mark from ChatGPT or yourself, even on an official task | Writing: four 0–5 bands per task. Speaking: five 0–5 bands (no Pronunciation) | No. Shown beside the anchor for orientation, never as a position on it |

Only two events in this plan produce official anchors: the diagnostic (Days 2–3) and Mock 2 (Day 20). Everything else is practice or tutor assessment. Speaking tutor bands are **not** converted to a mark out of 75, because Pronunciation (10 of the 75 marks) cannot be judged.

The one official reference point for Writing is the set of **examiner-marked candidate scripts** published with each sample paper (six per paper, marks from 9/20 to 17/20). After writing the same task you can place your text between them. That is a ranking against official marks, not a mark.

### What practice scores can and cannot tell us

- An **official** sample taken timed, in one sitting, gives a meaningful position relative to the 160/180/200 anchors for Reading, Use of English and Listening.
- **Writing and Speaking** practice marks come from ChatGPT or self-assessment, not a trained examiner. Treat them as rough and probably generous. They are useful for tracking direction, not level.
- **Unofficial tests** (Flo-Joe, engexam) have no validated conversion. Use percentages and trends only. Ignore any "score out of 210" a third-party site displays.
- EF SET reports a CEFR band on its own scale. It says nothing reliable about Cambridge task types such as key word transformations or gapped texts.

## 4. The digital exam

Sources: [Digital FAQ][faq], [sample vs live differences][diff], [prep page][prep] **[Confirmed]**.

| Feature | Fact |
|---|---|
| Navigation | Move freely within a paper; answers can be changed any time before the paper ends |
| Answered/unanswered | Bar above each question number turns black when answered, stays grey when not |
| Highlighting | Select text → right-click → Highlight |
| Notes | Select text → right-click → Note. Notes can be copied into answers |
| Timer | Top of screen. Shows minutes only until 5 minutes remain, then minutes and seconds with a colour change; turns red in the final minute. Can be hidden |
| Writing | Automatic word count; cut/copy/paste works. **No spell-checker** |
| Font | Size and colour adjustable |
| Paper | Scrap paper supplied by the centre; bring a pen or pencil |
| Listening | Headphones with on-screen volume. Cannot pause. No answer-transfer step; **2 minutes to check at the end** |
| Speaking | Face to face, same as paper-based |
| Results | **5–10 working days** (paper-based: 4–6 weeks) |

Free official digital sample (Inspera player) limitations: it **does not mark your answers**, you **cannot review them after finishing**, highlighting does not persist and notes do not work. Check answers against the key *before* closing each paper, and copy your Writing text out before finishing.

### Does digital need special preparation? Yes, a little

1. **Type every practice essay with spell-check and autocorrect off.** The exam has neither. Spelling errors count in Writing and make Use of English Part 2–4 and Listening Part 2 answers wrong.
2. **Keyboard layout risk.** A Zagreb centre may have Croatian QWERTZ keyboards (Z/Y swapped, apostrophe and quotation marks elsewhere). Not confirmed. Ask the centre (§9); if so, practise once on that layout.
3. **Read long texts on screen**, with scrolling and split panes, not on paper. Practise Parts 5–8 on a laptop.
4. **Listening Part 2** answers are typed while listening. Practise typing short answers without looking away from the questions for long.
5. **Use the highlight tool deliberately** in Parts 5–8 (mark the sentence that justifies each answer). Plan with scrap paper for Writing.
6. Learn the timer behaviour so the colour change at 5 minutes is not a surprise.

Otherwise the content, marking and difficulty are identical to paper.

## 6. B2 → C1: where marks are usually lost

Synthesis of Cambridge's handbook and teacher guides plus exam-teaching practice. **[Established / Estimate]**; the linked guides are the authority.

| Area | Typical B2-level behaviour | What C1 marking rewards |
|---|---|---|
| Use of English 1 | Choosing by meaning alone | Collocation, dependent prepositions, fixed phrases |
| Use of English 2 | Guessing content words | Grammar words: articles, prepositions, linkers, auxiliaries, relatives |
| Use of English 3 | Changing word class only | Negative prefixes, plurals, internal spelling changes, two-step changes |
| Use of English 4 | Paraphrasing loosely | Exact structure: inversion, passives, conditionals, wish/regret, modals of deduction, phrasal verbs. Key word unchanged, 3–6 words |
| Reading 5 | Matching words in text and option | Attitude, implication, purpose; distractors reuse text vocabulary |
| Reading 6 | Reading each text in isolation | Comparing four writers' opinions on the same point |
| Reading 7 | Filling gaps by topic | Reference words, linkers, chronology before and after the gap |
| Listening 1, 3 | Catching the fact | Speaker attitude, purpose, agreement |
| Listening 2 | Paraphrasing or misspelling | The exact word heard, correctly spelled, fitting the grammar |
| Listening 4 | Doing the two tasks one after another | Both tasks at once on first hearing, confirm on second |
| Writing | Correct but plain; one register; opinion essay habits from school | Addressing exactly two points, a clear line of argument, genre conventions (headings in reports/proposals), consistent register, a range of complex structures |
| Speaking | Long fluent monologues | Comparing and speculating in Part 2 rather than describing; genuinely responding to the partner in Part 3 |

Slovenian-speaker points to watch **[Estimate]**: articles (a/the/zero), present perfect versus past simple, prepositions after adjectives and verbs, comma before "that", and tense backshift in reported speech. The diagnostic will show which of these are real for you.

## 7. Learning strategy — evidence and how it changes the plan

Citations are from the literature as I know it; I did not re-fetch the papers this session.

| Finding | Source | Consequence for 26 days |
|---|---|---|
| Practice testing and distributed practice are the two highest-utility study techniques; rereading and highlighting are low-utility | Dunlosky et al. (2013), *Psychological Science in the Public Interest* | Every session is built on timed attempts with answers checked, not on reading tips |
| Retrieval strengthens memory more than restudy | Roediger & Karpicke (2006), *Psychological Science* | Mistake notebook is reviewed by covering the answer and producing it |
| Best review gap is roughly 10–20% of the time until the test | Cepeda et al. (2008), *Psychological Science* | Review intervals of 1, 3 and 7 days fit a 26-day horizon |
| Mixing problem types beats blocking for discrimination | Rohrer & Taylor (2007); Dunlosky et al. (2013) | Two or three different task types per day, never a whole day on one |
| Written corrective feedback works when it is focused and the writer revises | Bitchener & Ferris (2012) | Every essay follows write → diagnose → self-correct → rewrite; no essay ends at "feedback received" |
| Practice should resemble the test conditions | Transfer-appropriate processing (Morris, Bransford & Franks, 1977) | Typed, timed, on screen, spell-check off |
